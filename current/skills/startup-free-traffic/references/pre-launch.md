\# The Pre-Launch Phase

&#x20;

Read this when the user is pre-launch or within \~4 weeks of launching. The core insight: getting the first 100 users starts \*before\* launch. Founders who treat launch day as the start of distribution get a 48-hour spike and then silence.

&#x20;

\## Phase A: Weeks -8 to -4 (Foundation)

&#x20;

1\. \*\*Landing page + waitlist.\*\* A simple page stating the problem, the promise, and an email field. This is the capture mechanism every other pre-launch activity feeds. Reduce friction ruthlessly: fast load, one field, instant confirmation.

2\. \*\*Community seeding.\*\* Join the 2-3 communities where the ICP actually lives (subreddits, Slack/Discord groups, forums, WhatsApp groups). Participate helpfully with \*zero\* product mentions for at least 2-3 weeks. This builds the account history and relationships that make launch-time posts land instead of getting flagged as spam.

3\. \*\*Start founder-led content.\*\* Begin the build-in-public narrative now (see channel-playbooks.md §5) — the problem you're solving, why existing tools fail, what you're learning from customer conversations. The audience built here is the launch-day amplifier.

4\. \*\*Direct conversations.\*\* Personally contact 20-50 people who have the problem. Frame as learning, not selling. These conversations validate messaging, seed the waitlist, and often produce the first design partners.

\## Phase B: Weeks -4 to 0 (Launch prep)

&#x20;

1\. \*\*Directory groundwork.\*\* Submit to relevant niche directories and curated tool lists. Unlike a launch spike, directories send small sustained traffic and backlinks for months — and they're sources AI engines cite (see geo-aeo.md §4).

2\. \*\*Product Hunt prep\*\* (if launching there): build the hunter/supporter network, prepare assets (gallery, first comment, demo video), and schedule so the founder can engage every comment in the first hours of launch day.

3\. \*\*Warm the waitlist.\*\* 2-3 emails before launch: progress updates, a sneak peek, an early-access offer. A cold waitlist converts poorly.

4\. \*\*Line up launch-week content.\*\* Draft the posts for each channel in advance — launch day is for conversations, not writing.

\## Phase C: Launch week

&#x20;

1\. Launch across the prepared channels \*in sequence\*, not all in one hour — waitlist email first (they convert best and provide day-one activity), then Product Hunt / communities / social.

2\. In communities, post as a member sharing their work, not as a marketer: the story, the lessons, an ask for feedback. Respond to every single comment.

3\. Capture everything: launch traffic converts at maybe 2-5% — funnel the rest to the email list, not just a signup page.

\## Phase D: Weeks +1 to +4 (Avoid the post-launch cliff)

&#x20;

1\. Personally onboard/message every early user. At this scale, unscalable beats scalable.

2\. Convert launch momentum into a compounding channel: pick the 1-2 channels from the diagnosis and start the consistent rhythm (content, community, outreach).

3\. Publish the "what we learned from launch" post — it's authentic build-in-public material and often outperforms the launch itself.

\## Anti-patterns to warn about

&#x20;

\- Pouring everything into launch day and treating the traffic drop as failure — one day of traffic is not a distribution strategy.

\- Posting the product in communities the founder joined yesterday.

\- Building features during the pre-launch window instead of talking to future users.

\- Waitlist with no warming — collecting emails and going silent until launch.
