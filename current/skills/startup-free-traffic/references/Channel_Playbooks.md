# Channel Playbooks
 
Detailed playbooks for each free traffic method. Read only the sections relevant to the channels chosen in the diagnosis phase.
 
## Contents
- Cross-applicable: Lead magnets · Influencer partnerships · Community engagement · Product Hunt & directories · Founder-led content & build-in-public
- B2B focused: LinkedIn organic engine · Signal-based cold outreach · Guest posting & PR
- B2C focused: Viral loops & referrals · SEO & content (see also geo-aeo.md) · Short-form video

## Cross-Applicable (B2B & B2C)
 
### 1. The High-Value Lead Magnet
Create an irresistible, high-value asset (a comprehensive playbook, template, database, or original research) and offer it free in exchange for an email address.
- **Why it works:** Provides immediate value and builds a targeted email list for future conversion.
- **Playbook:**
  - The magnet must solve a specific, painful problem for the exact target audience — "ultimate guide to marketing" fails; "the 14-email sequence that took us from 0 to 500 demo calls" works.
  - Original data beats recycled advice: even a small survey of 50 target customers is citable, shareable, and unique.
  - Distribute the magnet through the other channels (LinkedIn posts, communities, guest posts) — a magnet with no distribution is a PDF in a drawer.
### 2. Influencer & Micro-Influencer Partnerships
Collaborate with individuals who already have the attention of the target audience.
- **Why it works:** Leverages existing trust and reach. Micro-influencers (10K-100K followers) typically deliver higher engagement than big names because their audiences trust them more, and they'll often work for product access or affiliate terms rather than cash.
- **Playbook:**
  - B2B: focus on LinkedIn thought leaders and niche newsletter authors. B2C: niche creators on Instagram, TikTok, or YouTube.
  - Offer exclusive access, co-created content, or affiliate incentives rather than upfront cash.
  - Prioritize fit over follower count: a creator whose audience exactly matches the ICP at 15K followers beats a generalist at 500K.
### 3. Community Engagement & "Dark Social"
Engage authentically in existing communities where the audience hangs out (Reddit, specialized Slack/Discord groups, Facebook/WhatsApp groups, niche forums).
- **Why it works:** Captures high-intent users in spaces where they actively seek solutions.
- **Playbook:**
  - The 80/20 rule: spend 80% of community time helping with zero product mentions, 20% (at most) mentioning the product when genuinely relevant. Announcements get ignored; useful contributions get noticed.
  - Track the questions that repeat — they are both content gold (for posts, SEO, lead magnets) and the exact language customers use.
  - Reddit punishes promotional intent hard; answer as a practitioner, disclose affiliation when linking.
  - For fragmented audiences spread across many small communities, pick the 2-3 most active and go deep rather than lurking in ten.
### 4. Product Hunt & Directory Launches
Launch on platforms designed for early adopters to discover new tools.
- **Why it works:** Drives a concentrated spike of high-intent traffic and social proof/backlinks.
- **Playbook:**
  - Build an audience *before* launching — prepare 3-4 weeks in advance, line up supporters, and engage every comment in the first hours of launch day.
  - Optimize for email capture rather than immediate sales; this is top-of-funnel traffic.
  - A launch is a spike measured in ~48 hours, not a strategy. Pair it with compounding channels.
  - Beyond Product Hunt: submit to niche directories and curated "tools" lists relevant to the category — individually small, but they keep sending trickle traffic and backlinks for months (which also feeds GEO, see geo-aeo.md).
### 5. Founder-Led Content & Build-in-Public
Build a consistent public presence as a founder — sharing the journey, decisions, lessons, and numbers — on the platform where the audience lives (LinkedIn for B2B, X/Threads/TikTok for B2C and dev tools).
- **Why it works:** People trust a person more than a faceless brand. Personal profiles typically generate several times more organic reach than company pages (commonly cited at ~2.75x, higher in some markets). Transparency attracts two audiences: other builders (who amplify) and early adopters (who want to join something unfinished but promising).
- **Playbook:**
  - Build a *narrative*, not a changelog. Tweeting every bug fix is noise; sharing why you made a pricing decision, what happened after a user churned, or what you learned from 10 failed cold DMs is a story people follow.
  - Consistency compounds: 3-4 posts per week for months, not a burst and a disappearance. Expect roughly 3-6 months before a visible audience forms — say this to the user upfront.
  - Use specifics as proof even before case studies exist: "We shipped this feature because 3 users asked" or "25 people now use this daily." Screenshots, feedback, and a public roadmap all count.
  - This channel pairs naturally with every other channel — it is the distribution layer for lead magnets, launches, and community presence.

## B2B Focused Methods
 
### 6. LinkedIn Organic Content Engine
The structured, tactical layer on top of founder-led content, specific to LinkedIn.
- **Why it works:** LinkedIn is the premier platform for B2B networking and organic reach, and its algorithm strongly favors personal profiles over company pages.
- **Playbook:**
  - Formats that perform: carousels/document posts, short actionable playbooks, behind-the-scenes building experiences, contrarian takes backed by data.
  - Embed links in comments or naturally within the content rather than the main text to avoid algorithmic penalties. CTAs (Follow + bell) go on the last slide of a carousel.
  - Commenting thoughtfully on larger accounts in the niche is a legitimate growth tactic — it borrows their audience.
### 7. Signal-Based Cold Outreach
Send highly targeted, personalized messages triggered by specific events (a company raising funds, a new executive hire, a specific job posting, a public complaint about a competitor).
- **Why it works:** Moves from spammy mass email to relevant, timely help. The signal provides both the reason to reach out and the personalization.
- **Playbook:**
  - Monitor signals with free/cheap tools: LinkedIn job posts, funding announcements, Google Alerts, community threads.
  - Message structure: name the signal → connect it to a specific pain → offer specific value (not "a quick call"). Keep it under 100 words.
  - Volume discipline: 10-20 genuinely personalized messages a day beat 500 templated ones — both in results and in sender reputation.
  - For very early stage, this doubles as customer discovery: even non-buyers teach you objections and language.
### 8. Strategic Guest Posting & PR
Write high-quality articles for authoritative industry blogs or publications.
- **Why it works:** Borrows authority and audience from established platforms while building SEO backlinks — and mentions in trusted publications are exactly what AI engines cite (see geo-aeo.md).
- **Playbook:**
  - Pitch unique data or contrarian viewpoints the publication's audience can't get elsewhere; recycled listicles get rejected.
  - Prioritize publications the ICP actually reads over generic high-DA sites.
  - Repurpose each guest post: LinkedIn thread, community answer, newsletter section.

## B2C Focused Methods
 
### 9. Viral Loops & Referral Mechanisms
Build features into the product that incentivize users to invite others.
- **Why it works:** Turns the existing user base into the marketing team.
- **Playbook:**
  - Tie the reward to the core value of the product (Dropbox: more storage for referrals) — cash rewards attract the wrong users.
  - Only force virality if the product genuinely has a sharing/collaboration mechanic; otherwise this wastes effort better spent on content or communities.
  - Measure the loop honestly: invite volume is a vanity metric. Track activation and retention of referred users vs. non-referred. A loop with high invites but weak activation is broken.
### 10. Programmatic & Content SEO
Create scalable content that answers specific search queries.
- **Why it works:** Long-term, compounding organic traffic — and increasingly, visibility in AI-generated answers.
- **Playbook:**
  - Focus on long-tail, pain-point keywords the ICP actually searches; build topic clusters to establish authority.
  - At early stage, prioritize 10-20 bottom-of-funnel pages answering painful, specific buying questions ("X vs Y", "best X for [niche]") over top-of-funnel volume.
  - Expect several months before meaningful traffic; this channel punishes impatience and rewards consistency.
  - **For everything related to AI search visibility (GEO/AEO) — read `geo-aeo.md`. In 2026 this is inseparable from SEO and often more impactful for new sites.**
### 11. Short-Form Video (TikTok / Reels / Shorts)
Leverage the algorithmic reach of short-form video platforms.
- **Why it works:** These platforms offer the highest organic reach potential for consumer products — the algorithm distributes based on content quality, not follower count, so a new account can go viral.
- **Playbook:**
  - Entertaining, educational, or highly relatable content showing the product solving a real problem — not commercials.
  - Volume and iteration: post frequently, watch which hooks retain viewers in the first 2 seconds, double down.
  - One video's traffic spike means little without a capture mechanism (link in bio → waitlist/lead magnet).
